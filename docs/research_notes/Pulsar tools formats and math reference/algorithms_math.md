## 1. Dispersion: delay, constant k_DM, smearing, effective width, DM-trial spacing

### Takeaway
The cold-plasma delay is $\Delta t = k_{\rm DM}\,{\rm DM}\,(f_1^{-2}-f_2^{-2})$ with the physical constant $k_{\rm DM}=e^2/(2\pi m_e c)=4.1488064239(11)\ {\rm GHz^2\,cm^3\,pc^{-1}\,ms}$ (= 4148.806 s MHz² pc⁻¹ cm³); the pulsar-community convention $k_{\rm DM}\equiv 1/(2.41\times10^{-4})=4149.378$ s MHz² pc⁻¹ cm³ is 0.0137% larger, so DMs quoted under the two conventions differ by that fraction. Effective time resolution adds sampling, intra-channel smearing, DM-error smearing, channel response and scattering in quadrature (Cordes & McLaughlin 2003), and DM trial spacing is chosen so the residual DM-error smearing is a fraction of that total.

### Cited Findings
- **Delay law.** $\tau(\nu)=t(\nu)-t(\infty)=\dfrac{e^2\,\mathrm{pc}}{2\pi m_e c}\,\dfrac{\rm DM}{\nu^2}$ (Kulkarni Eq. 4); for two frequencies $\Delta t = k_{\rm DM}\,{\rm DM}\,(f_1^{-2}-f_2^{-2})$, with DM $=\int_0^D n_e\,dl$ in pc cm⁻³. Assumes cold, tenuous, unmagnetised plasma, $\nu\gg\nu_p$. — [Kulkarni 2020, arXiv:2007.02886](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2007.02886)
- **Physical constant.** $a\equiv K^{-1}=\dfrac{e^2}{2\pi m_e c}\times\mathrm{pc}=\dfrac{1}{8\pi^2}\dfrac{e^2}{\epsilon_0 m_e c}\times{\rm pc}$ (Eqs. 6–7) $=4.148\,806\,4239(11)\ {\rm GHz^2\,cm^3\,pc^{-1}\,ms}$ (Eq. 8); $K=241.033\,1786(66)\ {\rm GHz^{-2}\,cm^{-3}\,pc\,s^{-1}}$ (Eq. 9). Unit conversion: $4.1488064\ {\rm GHz^2\,ms}=4148.8064\ {\rm s\,MHz^2}$. — [Kulkarni 2020](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2007.02886)
- **Conventional constants.** Kulkarni notes pulsar programs use $a=4.148\,808(3)\ {\rm GHz^2\,cm^3\,pc^{-1}\,ms}$ (Lorimer & Kramer 2004) or $a\equiv4.15$; the Manchester & Taylor (1972) convention is ${\rm DM}=2.41000\times10^{-16}\,\mathcal{D}$ (Hz), i.e. a *fixed* scaling constant, "with the slop taken up by" DM. — [Kulkarni 2020](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2007.02886)
- **The 1/2.41e-4 convention in software.** dspsr: ${\rm DM\,(pc\,cm^{-3})}=2.41\times10^{-4}\,D\ ({\rm s\,MHz^2})$ (Eq. 4), "the standard adopted by pulsar astronomers and implemented by dspsr", even though the constant "has been derived with greater precision (Backer et al. 1993)". Numerically $1/2.41\times10^{-4}=4149.378$ s MHz² pc⁻¹ cm³ (computed here), i.e. $1.37\times10^{-4}$ fractional above 4148.808. — [van Straten & Bailes 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.3973)
- **Kulkarni recommendation.** Report the measured "dispersion slope" $\mathcal{D}(\nu_1,\nu_2)=[t(\nu_1)-t(\nu_2)]/(\nu_1^{-2}-\nu_2^{-2})$ (Eq. 21) and derive ${\rm DM}=K\mathcal{D}$ (Eq. 22); report topocentric and barycentric values since $\mathcal{D}$ is not Lorentz invariant; use 8-byte floats, do not round constants. — [Kulkarni 2020](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2007.02886)
- **Effective time resolution (quadrature sum).** $\Delta t=\left(\Delta t_{\rm DM}^2+\Delta t_{\delta\rm DM}^2+\Delta t_{\Delta\nu}^2+\tau_d^2\right)^{1/2}$ (CM03 Eq. 2). Assumption: approximately Gaussian-like broadening components; "approximately the quadratic sum". — [Cordes & McLaughlin 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)
- **Intra-channel smearing.** $\Delta t_{\rm DM}=8.3\,\mu{\rm s}\ {\rm DM}\,\Delta\nu_{\rm MHz}\,\nu_{\rm GHz}^{-3}$ (CM03 Eq. 3; $\Delta\nu$ = channel width). Derivative of the delay law: $2k_{\rm DM}\Delta\nu/\nu^3 = 8.2976\,\mu$s for $k_{\rm DM}=4148.808$ (computed). Delay form used: $t_{\rm DM}=4.2\times10^3\,{\rm DM}\,\nu_{\rm GHz}^{-2}\,\mu$s (Eq. 7). — [CM03](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)
- **DM-error smearing.** $\Delta t_{\delta\rm DM}=\Delta t_{\rm DM}\,(\delta{\rm DM}/{\rm DM})$ (Eq. 4), evaluated with $\Delta\nu$ = total bandwidth. — [CM03](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)
- **Channel filter response.** $\Delta t_{\Delta\nu}\sim(\Delta\nu)^{-1}=(\Delta\nu_{\rm MHz})^{-1}\,\mu$s (Eq. 5). — [CM03](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)
- **Scattering (empirical mean).** $\log\tau_d=-3.72+0.411\log{\rm DM}+0.937(\log{\rm DM})^2-4.4\log\nu_{\rm GHz}\ \mu$s (Eq. 6), scatter $\sigma_{\log\tau_d}\approx0.65$; 4.4 assumes Kolmogorov turbulence (some LOS closer to 4.0). — [CM03](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)
- **Optimal channel width / minimum resolution.** Equating $\Delta t_{\rm DM}=\Delta t_{\Delta\nu}$ gives $\Delta\nu_{\rm MHz}=(\nu_{\rm GHz}^3/8.3{\rm DM})^{1/2}$; then $\Delta t_0=[2(\Delta t_{\rm DM}^{\min})^2+\tau_d^2]^{1/2}$ with $\Delta t_{\rm DM}^{\min}\approx(8.3\,{\rm DM}\,\nu_{\rm GHz}^{-3})^{1/2}\,\mu$s (Eqs. 8–9). Coherent dedispersion: $\Delta t_0=(\Delta t_{\Delta\nu}^2+\tau_d^2)^{1/2}$ (Eq. 10). — [CM03](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)
- **DM trial spacing.** Let residual $\Delta t_{\delta\rm DM}$ be a fraction $\epsilon$ of the total error: $\delta{\rm DM}/{\rm DM}=\epsilon\,\Delta t/\Delta t_{\rm DM}$ (Eq. 11, $\Delta t\to\Delta t_0$ for optimal resolution); spacing grows with DM because $\Delta t_{\rm DM}$ and $\tau_d$ grow. — [CM03](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)
- **Peak-flux loss for DM error** (rectangular band, Gaussian pulse FWHM $W$): $\dfrac{S(\delta{\rm DM})}{S}=\dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\zeta^{-1}{\rm erf}\,\zeta$, $\zeta=6.91\times10^{-3}\,\delta{\rm DM}\,\dfrac{\Delta\nu_{\rm MHz}}{W_{\rm ms}\,\nu_{\rm GHz}^3}$ (Eqs. 12–13); FWHM in $\zeta$ is $\approx3.5$, i.e. $\Delta{\rm DM}\approx506\,W_{\rm ms}\nu_{\rm GHz}^3/\Delta\nu_{\rm MHz}$ (Eq. 14). — [CM03](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)
- **FDMT view of DM spacing.** Number of distinguishable DM trials for brute force $N_\Delta=N_d/N_f$, where $N_d$ = dispersion sweep across band in samples; sensitivity-optimal channel count $N_f=\sqrt{N_d}$ (Eq. 7), from noise-to-pulse power ratio $\sqrt{N_f^2+N_d^2/N_f^2}/N_p$ (Eq. 6). — [Zackay & Ofek 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5373)

### Inferences
- DDplan-style logic (PRESTO `DDplan.py`) follows directly from CM03: total smearing $t_{\rm tot}=\sqrt{t_{\rm samp}^2+t_{\rm chan}^2+t_{\rm BW}^2(\delta{\rm DM})+t_{\rm sub}^2}$; choose $\delta{\rm DM}$ such that $t_{\rm BW}=k_{\rm DM}\,\delta{\rm DM}\,(f_{\rm lo}^{-2}-f_{\rm hi}^{-2})/2\lesssim t_{\rm samp}$ (half-step error), and downsample by factor 2 whenever $t_{\rm chan}$ at the current DM exceeds ~2× the current effective sample time. This is my reconstruction; the code was not opened.
- Using $1/2.41\times10^{-4}$ vs 4148.808 produces a $1.4\times10^{-4}$ fractional DM bias (e.g. DM 500 → 0.07 pc cm⁻³), below typical search DM steps but relevant for precision timing/catalogue comparison.

## 2. Dedispersion algorithms: brute force, subband, tree, FDMT, coherent

### Takeaway
Brute force costs $N_\Delta N_f N_t$; Taylor's tree costs $N_t N_f\log_2 N_f$ but assumes linear sweeps; FDMT is exact for the $\nu^{-2}$ law at $\max\{N_tN_\Delta\log_2N_f,\,2N_fN_t\}$ via a $\log_2 N_f$-step sub-band merge recursion. Coherent dedispersion multiplies the voltage spectrum by the inverse of $H(\nu_0+\nu)=\exp[i2\pi D\nu^2/(\nu_0^2(\nu_0+\nu))]$, whose impulse response (chirp) lasts $t_d=D(\nu_{\min}^{-2}-\nu_{\max}^{-2})$.

### Cited Findings
- **Complexities (Zackay & Ofek Table 1):** FDMT $\max\{N_tN_\Delta\log_2N_f,\ 2N_fN_t\}$; brute force $N_fN_tN_\Delta$; tree (Taylor 1974) $N_fN_t\log_2N_f$. Tree dedispersion "efficiently calculates" sums along straight-line (linear) dispersion paths, so is approximate for $\nu^{-2}$ curves; brute force is "exact, but has the high complexity of $N_\Delta N_fN_t$" (GPU real-time, Barsdell et al. 2012). — [Zackay & Ofek 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5373)
- **FDMT recursion.** Define $A_{f_0}^{f_2}(t_0,\Delta t)$ = sum of $I(f,t)$ along the unique dispersion curve between $(t_0,f_0)$ and $(t_0-\Delta t,f_2)$. Iteration $i=1..\log_2 N_f$, sub-band $[f_0,f_2]$ with $f_2=f_0+2^i\delta f$, $f_1=(f_0+f_2)/2$:
  $C_{f_2,f_0}\equiv\dfrac{f_1^{-2}-f_0^{-2}}{f_2^{-2}-f_0^{-2}}$ (Eq. 16), $t_1=t_0-C_{f_2,f_0}\Delta t$,
  $A_{f_0}^{f_2}(t_0,\Delta t)=A_{f_0}^{f_1}(t_0,t_0-t_1)+A_{f_1}^{f_2}(t_1,t_1-t_2)$; if $t_1<0$ copy: $A_{f_0}^{f_2}(t_0,\Delta t)=A_{f_0}^{f_1}(t_0,t_0-t_1)$ (Eq. 17).
  Max delay per sub-band $\Delta t_{\max}(i,f_0)=N_\Delta\dfrac{f_0^{-2}-(f_0+2^i\delta f)^{-2}}{f_{\min}^{-2}-f_{\max}^{-2}}$ (Eq. 22). Initialisation $A_f^{f+\delta f}(t,\Delta t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\Delta t}I(f,t+i)$ (Eq. 20), or $=I(f,t)$ if per-channel delay < one bin (Eq. 18), $\delta f=(f_{\max}-f_{\min})/N_f$. — [Zackay & Ofek 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5373)
- **FDMT cost derivation.** Under near-linear sweep, iteration $i$ needs $\Delta t_{\max}(i)\approx N_\Delta2^i/N_f$, bands $N_b=N_f/2^i$, so $N_b\Delta t_{\max}(i)\approx\max\{N_\Delta,N_b\}$ (Eq. 25) giving $C_{\rm FDMT}\le2N_tN_f+N_tN_\Delta\log_2N_f$ (Eq. 28) $=2N_s+\frac{N_sN_d}{N_f^2}\log_2N_f$ (Eq. 29, $N_s=N_fN_t$, $N_\Delta=N_d/N_f$). The dispersion kernel is power preserving, $|\hat H(f)|^2=1$. — [Zackay & Ofek 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5373)
- **Coherent dedispersion transfer function.** $H(\nu+\nu_0)=\exp\!\left[i\dfrac{2\pi D\nu^2}{\nu_0^2(\nu+\nu_0)}\right]$ (Eq. 3), $\nu_0$ = centre frequency, $\nu$ = offset within band, used to deconvolve the voltage signal (Hankins 1971; Hankins & Rickett 1975); $D$ from ${\rm DM}=2.41\times10^{-4}D$ [s MHz²]. Chirp / smearing time $t_d=D(\nu_{\min}^{-2}-\nu_{\max}^{-2})$ (Eq. 5). Overlap-save: discard $n_d=n_d^-+n_d^+$ wrap-around points per FFT segment with $n_d^\pm=r\,t_d^\pm$ ($r$ = sampling rate), where $t_d^{\pm}$ are smearing in upper/lower half-band; FFT length must exceed $n_d$ (efficiency improves with $N\gg n_d$). — [van Straten & Bailes 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.3973)
- CM03: the coherent-dedispersion deconvolution kernel is "approximately in the form of a 'chirp' function having duration equal to the dispersion time across the total bandwidth" (Eq. 3 with $\Delta\nu$ = total BW); implementable with FFTs (Hankins 1971) or FIR time-domain filters. — [CM03](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)

### Inferences
- Subband (two-stage) dedispersion: stage 1 dedisperses $N_f$ channels into $N_{\rm sub}$ subbands at $N_{\rm coarse}$ nominal DMs, stage 2 dedisperses $N_{\rm sub}$ subbands to all $N_\Delta$ fine DMs within each coarse step; cost $\approx N_t(N_fN_{\rm coarse}+N_{\rm sub}N_\Delta)$ vs $N_tN_fN_\Delta$, at the cost of an additional smearing term $t_{\rm sub}\approx k_{\rm DM}\,\delta{\rm DM}_{\rm coarse}\,(f_{\rm sub,lo}^{-2}-f_{\rm sub,hi}^{-2})/2$ (my derivation; consistent with CM03 Eq. 4 applied per subband).
- Sign of the exponent in $H$ depends on FFT sign convention and upper/lower sideband; implementations conjugate $H$ to dedisperse.

## 3. RFI statistics: rfifind-style, zero-DM, spectral kurtosis, IQRM, MAD

### Takeaway
SK compares the second moment of power to the square of the first; for $M$ unaveraged power spectra $\widehat{SK}=\frac{M+1}{M-1}(M S_2/S_1^2-1)$ has mean 1 and variance $\approx 4/M$, generalised to $\frac{MNd+1}{M-1}(\cdot)$ for $N$-averaged spectra (Nita & Gary 2010). IQRM flags channels using lagged differences of a per-channel statistic, robust location/scale via median and IQR/1.349, a $t\sigma$ fence, and a voting graph. Zero-DM subtracts the across-band mean per time sample, acting as a high-pass filter on dispersed signals.

### Cited Findings
- **Original SK estimator** ($N=d=1$): $\widehat{SK}=\dfrac{M+1}{M-1}\left[\dfrac{M S_2}{S_1^2}-1\right]$, $S_1=\sum_{i=1}^M P_i$, $S_2=\sum P_i^2$ over $M$ instantaneous PSD estimates; unbiased with expectation 1 for Gaussian (RFI-free) signal. Variance (from GSK formula with $N=d=1$): $4M^2/[(M-1)(M+2)(M+3)]\to4/M$. — [Nita & Gary 2010, arXiv:1005.4371](https://arxiv.org/html/1005.4371v1)
- **Generalised SK** (Eq. 8): $\widehat{SK}=\dfrac{MNd+1}{M-1}\left[\dfrac{MS_2}{S_1^2}-1\right]$, $S_1=\sum_{i=1}^M\left(\sum_{j=1}^N x_j\right)_i$, $S_2=\sum_{i=1}^M\left(\sum_{j=1}^Nx_j\right)_i^2$; $x_j$ gamma-distributed with shape $d$ ($d=1$ for one complex FFT power, $d=1/2$ for real-valued). $E[\widehat{SK}]=1$; $\mu_2=\dfrac{2Nd(Nd+1)M^2\Gamma(MNd+2)}{(M-1)\Gamma(MNd+4)}$ (Eq. 9) $\simeq\frac{2}{M}[1+1/(dN)]$ for large $N$. — [Nita & Gary 2010](https://arxiv.org/html/1005.4371v1)
- **SK thresholds.** Asymmetric thresholds at false-alarm 0.13499% per tail ("same as a 3σ"), from Pearson Type IV (or VI) fits, or the simpler Type III (shifted gamma: $\alpha=\mu_3/2\mu_2$, $\beta=4\mu_2^3/\mu_3^2$, $\delta=1-2\mu_2^2/\mu_3$). Example $N=10,M=300,d=1$: Type III [0.76754, 1.28212] vs symmetric $1\pm3\sqrt{\mu_2}$ [0.74288, 1.25712]. — [Nita & Gary 2010](https://arxiv.org/html/1005.4371v1)
- **IQRM lagged differences.** $\Delta_i^k=x_i-x_{i-k}$, with edge clamping; $x_i$ = per-channel statistic (e.g. channel std, or |ACF lag-1|) where larger = more RFI. Lags in $[-r,r]\setminus\{0\}$ via $k_0=1$, $k_{m+1}=\max(\lfloor1.5k_m\rfloor,k_m+1)$ plus negatives (r=10 → ±1,2,3,4,6,9). — [Morello, Rajwade & Stappers 2022, MNRAS 510, 1393](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.12434)
- **IQRM robust scale and fence.** $\mu_k=Q_k(0.5)$, $\sigma_k=\dfrac{Q_k(0.75)-Q_k(0.25)}{\Phi^{-1}(0.75)-\Phi^{-1}(0.25)}\approx{\rm IQR}/1.349$; flag $x_i$ high vs $x_{i-k}$ if $\Delta_i^k-\mu_k>t\,\sigma_k$ (one-sided Tukey-style fence in Gaussian sigmas). Voting: $(i-k)\to i$; a vote $i\to j$ is valid iff $i$ cast strictly fewer votes than $j$ received; any index with ≥1 valid vote is masked. Used parameters $t=3$, $r\approx10\%$ of channel count (MeerKAT r=100; Lovell r=67 of 672). ~100× faster than real time on one core (Lovell); cost dominated by computing the spectral statistic. — [Morello et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.12434)
- **Zero-DM filter.** Eatough, Keane & Lyne (2009, MNRAS 395, 410) subtract, per time sample, the band-averaged signal (the DM = 0 time series) from every channel, removing broadband undispersed RFI; analysed as a high-pass filter on dispersed pulses with low-frequency cutoff $\approx500/{\rm DM}$ Hz. — [Eatough et al. 2009, arXiv:0901.3993](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0901.3993); [journal record](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/an-interference-removal-technique-for-radio-pulsar-searches/)

### Inferences
- Zero-DM in formula form: $x'(t,f)=x(t,f)-\frac{1}{N_f}\sum_{f'}x(t,f')$ (possibly bandpass-weighted). A dispersed pulse of sweep $t_{\rm sweep}$ contributes only $\sim W/t_{\rm sweep}$ of its amplitude to the mean in any sample, so loss is small unless DM is low — consistent with the 500/DM Hz cutoff.
- MAD: $\hat\sigma=1.4826\,{\rm median}(|x-{\rm median}(x)|)$; $1.4826=1/\Phi^{-1}(0.75)$, analogous to IQR/1.349 (since IQR $=2\times0.6745\sigma$). These are standard Gaussian consistency constants (my statement; see Rousseeuw & Croux 1993, JASA 88, 1273).
- rfifind-style (PRESTO): data split into blocks of $\sim$seconds per channel; for each (block, channel) compute mean, std and the max normalised FFT power; flag cells whose mean or std deviate by more than `-timesig` (σ) from robust global statistics or whose max power exceeds `-freqsig`; then flag whole channels/intervals if flagged fraction exceeds `-chanfrac` / `-intfrac`. Defaults recalled as timesig=10, freqsig=4, chanfrac=0.7, intfrac=0.3 — not verified against source.

## 4. Single-pulse detection: boxcar matched filter, S/N, width mismatch, clustering, false alarms

### Takeaway
Optimal detection of a pulse of area $A_i$ and width $W_i$ gives $(S/N)_i=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{W_n}}\frac{A_i}{\sqrt{W_i}}$; boxcar S/N for width $w$ samples is $\sum_{w}(x-\mu)/(\sigma\sqrt{w})$, and broadening to $W_b$ reduces S/N by $(W_i/W_b)^{1/2}$. False-alarm counts follow Gaussian tails times the number of independent (t, DM, width) trials.

### Cited Findings
- **Matched-filter S/N.** $(S/N)_i=\dfrac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{W_n}}\dfrac{A_i}{\sqrt{W_i}}$ (CM03 Eq. 15), $\sigma$ = rms per sample, $W_n$ = noise correlation time, $W_i\gg W_n$; radiometer $\sigma=S_{\rm sys}/(N_{\rm pol}\Delta\nu W_n)^{1/2}$; so S/N $\propto A_i/\sqrt{W_i}$, independent of $W_n$. — [CM03](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)
- **Broadening loss.** If broadening (scattering, DM error, orbital motion) yields width $W_b$, matched filtering gives $(S/N)_b=(S/N)_i(W_i/W_b)^{1/2}$ (Eq. 16). Smoothing by iterative addition of adjacent samples is "a straight-forward and efficient approximation to optimal detection". — [CM03](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)
- **Thresholding / false alarms.** Gaussian noise statistics assumed ("good assumption when the time-bandwidth product ≳5"); number of detections per S/N bin of size $\Delta$ is $\Delta N_p\approx2NN_{\rm DM}\Delta f(S/N)$ (Eq. 18), $f$ = Gaussian PDF, $N$ samples, $N_{\rm DM}$ trials; only the highest S/N across smoothings is recorded. — [CM03](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0304364)
- **Boxcar S/N with baseline (profile/fold version).** $S/N=\max_{w,p}\dfrac{S_w(p)-\delta S_{\rm tot}}{\sigma\sqrt{w(1-\delta)}}$, $\delta=w/n$, $S_w(p)=\sum_{i=0}^{w-1}P_{(p+i)\bmod n}$ (Eq. 3); the $\sqrt{1-\delta}$ factor accounts for subtracting the mean estimated from the same $n$ bins. Each $(w,p)$ term is unit-variance Gaussian under white noise. Boxcars via prefix sums $S_w(p)=C_{p+w}-C_p$. — [Ransom 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11749); prefix-sum trick from [Morello et al. 2020 Eq. 37–38](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03701)
- **Boxcar vs Gaussian efficiency.** A boxcar of the right width recovers ~94% of the matched-filter S/N of a Gaussian pulse; with widths spaced by factor 2, average efficiency for an unknown-width Gaussian is $E\approx0.930$ (Morello Eq. 25). — [Ransom 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11749); [Morello et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03701)

### Inferences
- Time-series form: $S/N_w(t)=\dfrac{\sum_{j=t}^{t+w-1}(x_j-\mu)}{\sigma\sqrt{w}}$ with $\mu,\sigma$ from robust (median/MAD or IQR) estimates after detrending (running median of window ≫ max width) — the $\delta\to0$ limit of Ransom Eq. 3.
- Width mismatch for a top-hat pulse of width $W$ searched with boxcar $W_b$: S/N ratio $=\min(W,W_b)/\sqrt{WW_b}$ (derived; e.g. factor-2 mismatch → 0.707, which is why Morello's ≈71% figure ($1/\sqrt2$) appears for a double-peaked pulse matched by a single-peak filter).
- False-alarm budget: $N_{\rm FA}\approx N_{\rm eff}\,\tfrac12{\rm erfc}(\eta/\sqrt2)$ with $N_{\rm eff}\approx\sum_{\rm DM}\sum_w N_t/w$ (independent trials per width); e.g. $\eta=6$ gives $9.9\times10^{-10}$ per trial.
- Clustering: friends-of-friends links candidates within $(\Delta t,\Delta{\rm DM},\Delta w)$ tolerances (as in heimdall); DBSCAN (Ester et al. 1996) adds a min-points density criterion; both group the S/N-vs-DM "bow" of one event into one candidate. No primary clustering paper was retrieved.

## 5. Periodicity: FFT normalisation, red noise, interpolation, harmonic sums, acceleration/jerk, FFA, coherent harmonic summing

### Takeaway
Normalised FFT powers are exponential ($\chi^2_2/2$), sums of $m$ harmonics are $\chi^2_{2m}/2$; median normalisation (divide by local median/ln 2) handles red noise; interbinning cuts the worst scalloping loss from 36% to 7.4% in amplitude. Acceleration searches correlate with templates in $(r,z)$, $z=\dot f T^2=a h f T^2/c$, jerk adds $w=\ddot fT^3$. The FFA is provably near-optimal (efficiency ≈0.93 with boxcars) at $O(N\log N)$ per octave; Ransom's 2026 coherent harmonic summing reconstructs profiles from ~60 interpolated complex harmonics and beat riptide and accelsearch in injections.

### Cited Findings
- **Power distribution.** Pure noise DFT powers are exponential (χ² with 2 dof) with mean and std $N\langle d_j^2\rangle$; normalised $P=|A_k|^2/(N\langle d_j^2\rangle)$ gives ${\rm Prob}(P\ge P')=e^{-P'}$ (Eq. 10). Sum of $m$ normalised powers: ${\rm Prob}(P_m\ge P')=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{(P')^j}{j!}e^{-P'}$ (Eq. 11) = prob that $\chi^2_{2m}>2P'$. — [Ransom, Eikenberry & Middleditch 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0204349)
- **Red noise / local normalisation.** $P_{k,\rm norm}=|A_k|^2/(N\langle d_j^2\rangle)\simeq P_k/P_{\rm local}$ (Eq. 12). Use local *median*: unit exponential has median $\ln2$, so divide by (local median)/ln 2, robust to strong narrow-band signals. — [REM02](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0204349)
- **Scalloping.** Non-integer frequency $r$: $A_k=A_0e^{-i\pi(k-r)}{\rm sinc}[\pi(k-r)]$, $P_k=P_0{\rm sinc}^2[\pi(k-r)]$ (Eq. 18c–d); worst case $|k-r|=1/2$ gives $|A_k|=2|A_0|/\pi$ ("nearly 60% loss of signal power"), average ~23% power loss. — [REM02](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0204349)
- **Fourier interpolation.** $A_r\simeq\sum_{k=r-m/2}^{r+m/2}A_ke^{-i\pi(r-k)}{\rm sinc}[\pi(r-k)]$ (Eq. 30) — a correlation of local FFT bins with the sinc template. **Interbinning:** $A_{k+1/2}\simeq\frac{\pi}{4}(A_k-A_{k+1})$ (Eq. 31, van der Klis 1989), reducing max S/N (amplitude) loss from $1-2/\pi\approx36\%$ to ~7.4% at offset $\pm(1-\pi/4)\approx0.215$ bin (amplitude 0.926); interbins have different noise properties and are not independent trials. — [REM02](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0204349)
- **Acceleration.** $\alpha=\dfrac{\dot f c}{hf}=\dfrac{zc}{hfT^2}$ (AR18 Eq. 1), $r=fT$, $z=\dot r=\dot fT^2$ (bins drifted during obs.), so $z=\alpha hfT^2/c=a\,h\,T^2/(cP)$. Constant acceleration assumed valid for $T\lesssim P_{\rm orb}/10$. **Jerk:** $\dot\alpha=\ddot fc/(hf)=wc/(hfT^3)$ (Eq. 2), $w=\dot z=\ddot fT^3$; signal model $n(u)=a\cos[2\pi(r_0u+\frac{z_0}{2}u^2+\frac{w}{6}u^3)+\phi]$, $u=t/T$; templates are complex conjugates of the numerically computed Fourier response, correlated over $\Delta r$; jerk search helps when $T\approx5$–15% of $P_{\rm orb}$. — [Andersen & Ransom 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07900)
- **Trials.** For $m$ summed harmonics, $N_{\rm trials}\sim\dfrac{N_rN_z}{6.95\,m}\left(\dfrac{N_w}{44.2}\right)$ (AR18 Eq. 6); harmonic sums of normalised powers are χ² with 2m dof. — [AR18](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07900)
- **Correlation technique.** Recover the coherent response by correlating local Fourier amplitudes with a "frequency-reversed and complex-conjugated template"; example grid (NGC 6544 search) $\Delta r=0.0625$ and $\Delta\dot r=0.25$, computed with short FFTs/overlap-save. — [REM02](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0204349)
- **FFA optimality.** Optimal test for known template: fold and correlate with template ($Z=x\cdot s$, Karlin–Rubin). Mismatched template: efficiency $E=s\cdot s'$, upper limit $U'_z=U_z/E$ (Eqs. 22–24); boxcar widths ×2 → $E_{\rm FFA}\approx0.930$, $U_{\rm FFA}\approx1.075\,\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)$ (Eq. 26). FFT+IHS: $I(a,h)=\sum|X_{k_0i}|^2$ (unitary DFT) is noncentral χ² with $\lambda=a^2P(h)$ (Eq. 32); $U_{\rm FFT}=\min_{h\le32}U_I(h)$ (Eq. 36); FFA more sensitive at every period and duty cycle, more so for short duty cycles; interbinning adds a further ÷1.074 to FFT efficiency. — [Morello et al. 2020, MNRAS 498, 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03701)
- **FFA cost.** Brute-force folding transform $O(pm^2)$ for $m$ rows of $p$ bins; FFA (Staelin 1969; depth-first in riptide) $mp\log_2 m$; per octave $C=bN\log_2N$, total time-series search $O(bN\log_2N)$ (Eqs. 39–40), $b$ = min bins. Data downsampled by octaves $[2^kP_0,2^{k+1}P_0]$. — [Morello et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03701)
- **Coherent harmonic summing (Ransom 2026, CoherentSearch.jl, Julia).** Input: FFT normalised to unit mean amplitude; at each trial $r$ interpolate complex amplitudes of $H$ harmonics (typically 60), stack as half-complex spectrum with DC=0, inverse real FFT to $n=2H$-bin profile (zero mean by construction), boxcar bank (×1.5 spacing, 1 bin to 30% duty), S/N via Eq. 3. Decimation: every $k$-th harmonic gives the profile at $k\times$ the trial frequency for one short iFFT. Because profile bins are band-limited (correlated), exact boxcar variance $\mathrm{var}(S_w)=2\sum_{j\ {\rm filled},\,j<n/2}|D_j|^2+\tfrac12|D_{n/2}|^2$, $D_j=\sin(\pi jw/n)/\sin(\pi j/n)$ (Eq. 4); differs from $\sigma\sqrt{w(1-\delta)}$ by $\approx1+3/(4n)$. Injection study (>860,000 pulsars, matched FAR): 76% recovered vs 64% riptide FFA vs 42% PRESTO accelsearch; threshold unchanged in red noise; 1.4–2.2× faster than riptide single-core, up to 27× on 48 cores, GPU up to 9× a 20-core workstation. arXiv v1 8 Oct 2026, to AJ. — [Ransom 2026, arXiv:2610.11749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11749)

### Inferences
- Time-domain resampling (acceleration correction by stretching time): $t'=t\,(1+a t/(2c))$ approx., i.e. resample with $\tau(t)=t+\frac{a}{2c}t^2$ before FFT; equivalent to the $z$ template correlation in the Fourier domain. (Standard; L&K §6.2 not retrieved.)
- Gaussian-equivalent significance of a harmonic sum: $p=Q(\chi^2_{2m}>2P_m)$, multiply by $N_{\rm trials}$, convert via $\sigma=\Phi^{-1}(1-p)$.

## 6. Folding: phase model, profile χ², H-test, S/N, P/Ṗ/DM optimisation

### Takeaway
Fold with $\phi(t)=\phi_0+f(t-t_0)+\frac12\dot f(t-t_0)^2+\frac16\ddot f(t-t_0)^3$; significance via reduced χ² of the profile against a flat model (prepfold) or Z²_m/H-test (photon/unbinned), with $H=\max_m(Z_m^2-4m+4)$ and $P(>H)=e^{-0.4H}$ (de Jager & Büsching 2010).

### Cited Findings
- **H-test.** $Z_m^2=2\pi N\psi(\hat f_m)$, equivalently $Z_m^2=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^m\left[(\sum_i\cos k\theta_i)^2+(\sum_i\sin k\theta_i)^2\right]$; $H\equiv\max_{1\le m\le20}(Z_m^2-4m+4)$ (Eq. 4); revised distribution ${\rm Prob}(>H)=\exp(-0.4H)$ (Eq. 5; λ=0.398) valid to large H (old DSR 1989 tables had a hard tail for H>23); $H\approx1.9X^2$ with $X=p\sqrt N$ the Gaussian significance. — [de Jager & Büsching 2010, arXiv:1005.4867](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.4867)
- **Profile boxcar S/N** after folding: Ransom 2026 / Morello 2020 Eq. 3 form above. — [Ransom 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11749)
- Folding across $p$ trial periods spanning one sample drift is exactly the FFA's folding transform; "integration paths with non-integer values of s are identical to that with the closest integer value", giving the finest period resolution allowed by sampling. — [Morello et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03701)

### Inferences
- Reduced χ² (PRESTO prepfold): $\chi^2_{\rm red}=\frac{1}{n_b-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n_b}\frac{(p_j-\bar p)^2}{\sigma_p^2}$, $\sigma_p^2=N_{\rm samp/bin}\sigma^2$ (white noise); under H0 $\sim\chi^2_{n_b-1}/(n_b-1)$. Optimisation over $(P,\dot P,{\rm DM})$: shift sub-integration/sub-band profiles by $\Delta\phi_{ij}=\delta f\,t_i+\frac12\delta\dot f\,t_i^2+f\,k_{\rm DM}\delta{\rm DM}(\nu_j^{-2}-\nu_{\rm ref}^{-2})$ and maximise χ² — standard, not line-verified.
- Phase drift tolerance: required step $\delta f\lesssim1/(n_bT)$, $\delta\dot f\lesssim2/(n_bT^2)$ (one bin of drift).

## 7. Timing: Fourier-domain template matching, TOA error, polycos, barycentric delays

### Takeaway
TOAs come from fitting a template shift in the Fourier domain (Taylor 1992), uncertainty $\propto W/({\rm S/N})$; arrival times are converted to the SSB via $\Delta_\odot=\Delta_A+\Delta_{R\odot}+\Delta_p+\Delta_{D\odot}+\Delta_{E\odot}+\Delta_{S\odot}$ (Tempo2).

### Cited Findings
- **Barycentric terms** (Tempo2 Eq. 8): $\Delta_\odot=\Delta_A+\Delta_{R\odot}+\Delta_p+\Delta_{D\odot}+\Delta_{E\odot}+\Delta_{S\odot}$ (atmospheric, Roemer, parallax, dispersion, Einstein, Shapiro). **Roemer:** $\Delta_{R\odot}=-\mathbf r\cdot\hat{\mathbf R}_{BB}/c$ (Eq. 13), $\hat R_0=(\cos\alpha\cos\delta,\sin\alpha\cos\delta,\sin\delta)$ plus proper-motion terms. **Einstein:** time-dilation integral $\Delta_{E\odot\oplus}=\frac{1}{c^2}\int^t\left(U_\oplus+\frac{v_\oplus^2}{2}+\Delta L_C^{(PN)}+\Delta L_C^{(A)}\right)dt$ (Eq. 9; Irwin & Fukushima 1999, DE405). **Shapiro:** $\Delta_{S\odot}=-2\sum_j\frac{Gm_j}{c^3}\ln|\hat R\cdot\mathbf r_j+|\mathbf r_j||=-\sum_j\frac{2Gm_j}{c^3}\ln[|\mathbf r_j|(1-\cos\psi_j)]$ (Eqs. 32–33), Sun, Venus, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. — [Edwards, Hobbs & Manchester 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0607664)
- Magnitudes (from same framework): Roemer up to ~500 s (1 AU/c), solar Shapiro up to ~120 µs near the limb ($2GM_\odot/c^3\approx9.85\,\mu$s × log term) — **my arithmetic, not quoted**.

### Inferences
- **Taylor (1992) Fourier-domain TOA** [standard form, source text not retrieved]: model profile $p(t)=a+b\,s(t-\tau)+n(t)$; with DFTs $P_k=|P_k|e^{i\phi_k}$, $S_k=|S_k|e^{i\theta_k}$, minimise $\chi^2(b,\tau)=\sum_{k=1}^{n/2}\frac{|P_k-bS_ke^{-i2\pi k\tau/N}|^2}{\sigma^2}=\sum\frac{|P_k|^2+b^2|S_k|^2-2b|P_k||S_k|\cos(\phi_k-\theta_k+2\pi k\tau/N)}{\sigma^2}$; $\hat b=\sum|P_k||S_k|\cos(\cdot)/\sum|S_k|^2$; $\sigma_\tau^2=\frac{\sigma^2}{2b\sum k^2|P_k||S_k|\cos(\cdot)}\left(\frac{N}{2\pi}\right)^2$. Gives sub-bin precision without interpolation. Citation: Taylor J. H. 1992, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 341, 117 — https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992RSPTA.341..117T
- **TOA uncertainty (radiometer):** $\sigma_{\rm TOA}\approx W/({\rm S/N})\propto\frac{S_{\rm sys}}{\sqrt{t_{\rm obs}\Delta\nu}}\frac{P\,\delta^{3/2}}{S_{\rm mean}}$ (Lorimer & Kramer 2005 Eq. 8.2 form; not line-verified).
- **Polyco expansion (TEMPO)** [standard form, not retrieved]: ${\rm phase}(t)={\rm RPHASE}+60\,\Delta T\,F_0+\sum_{i=1}^{N}C_i\,\Delta T^{i-1}$, $f(t)=F_0+\frac{1}{60}\sum_{i=2}^N(i-1)C_i\Delta T^{i-2}$, $\Delta T$ in minutes from TMID; doc: https://tempo.sourceforge.net/ref_man_sections/tz-polyco.txt
- Timing phase model: $\phi(t)=\phi_0+\nu(t-t_0)+\frac12\dot\nu(t-t_0)^2+\dots$ evaluated at barycentric time $t=t_{\rm obs}-\Delta_\odot-\Delta_{\rm binary}$; dispersion delay referenced to infinite frequency using $k_{\rm DM}$ (choice of constant must match between search and timing software).
